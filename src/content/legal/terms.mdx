## 10. Response actions on your Endpoints

### 10.1 Your authorization

The Direwolf AI SOC acts on your Endpoints without waiting for a human when its confidence is high. **By enabling the Direwolf AI SOC you authorize Direwolf, acting through its automated systems and its analysts, to take the containment actions permitted by your Response Policy on your Endpoints, including killing processes, quarantining files and isolating hosts from the network, and you acknowledge that these actions can interrupt business operations and that an isolated host may lose network connectivity until it is reconnected.** Actions taken in accordance with your Response Policy are authorized access to your Endpoints for the purposes of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and comparable state laws.

### 10.2 Your Response Policy and guardrails

The Response Policy is yours to configure. It includes toggles for each action type, confidence thresholds, a list of hosts the AI may never isolate on its own (for example domain controllers and hosts you tag as critical), a cap on automatic isolations per hour, and a cool-down that prevents a host from being isolated and reconnected repeatedly. Direwolf also maintains a platform-wide switch that drops the AI to recommend-only mode. **You are responsible for keeping the never-isolate list and critical-host tags current.** We will tell you when you first enable the Direwolf AI SOC what the default policy is.

### 10.3 Records, notification and undo

Every automated action is recorded in your audit log with the AI's reasoning, your administrators are notified when actions are taken, and every action can be reversed with one click (reconnect host, restore file) from the Platform. Your administrators can also report a false positive, which reverses the verdict and records the correction.

### 10.4 Your authority over Endpoints

You represent that you own or control every Endpoint on which you install a sensor, that you have authority to permit the actions in this section on it, and that you have informed its users as your policies and applicable law require.

### 10.5 Allocation of responsibility
