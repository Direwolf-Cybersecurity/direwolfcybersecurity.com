### 3.4 In penetration testing engagements

When your organization engages us for a penetration test, we receive the information it gives us to plan and run the engagement: its contacts for the engagement, the targets in scope and any test accounts it provides. During testing we may come across other information held on the systems in scope. We use this information only to perform the engagement and prepare its report, and we treat it as your organization's confidential information under our [Terms of Service](/legal/terms/).

## 5. AI processing

The Direwolf AI SOC uses a large language model to analyze security alerts. AI analysis runs on Amazon Bedrock in AWS US Regions: Bedrock's US inference profile processes each request in US East (N. Virginia), US East (Ohio) or US West (Oregon). Your alert data is not sent to third-party AI services and is not used to train models. Amazon Web Services states that content processed through Amazon Bedrock is not used to improve the underlying models and is not shared with model providers.

Every automated verdict is labeled as automated in the Platform ("Direwolf SOC (automated)"). The AI SOC's work is monitored by a team of security professionals, who take the cases it cannot decide.

## 9. How we protect information

In plain language, this is how the Platform protects your data. A fuller description is on our [Platform](/platform/#security) page, and our compliance status, including what we do and do not claim, is on our [Compliance](/compliance/) page.

- **Hosting.** The Platform runs on Amazon Web Services in the US West (Oregon) Region, with customer data stored in the United States. The Platform is being brought into service; these statements describe how customer data is handled once you use it.
- **Encryption.** Data in transit is protected with TLS 1.2 or higher, and data at rest (databases, object storage, queues, secrets and logs) is encrypted with customer-managed keys in AWS Key Management Service, with automatic key rotation. The FIPS 140 validation details for these components are on our [Compliance](/compliance/) page.
- **Multi-factor authentication** is mandatory for every user: an authenticator app or a passkey, plus recovery codes. Direwolf staff must use a passkey or hardware security key.
- **Tenant isolation.** Each customer organization is isolated at the database layer with PostgreSQL row-level security, enforced for every query, in addition to application-level permission checks.
- **Audit logging.** Every administrative and security-relevant action is written to an append-only audit log with a tamper-evident hash chain. Organization administrators can review their own organization's log.
- **Sessions and accounts.** Sessions lock after 15 minutes of inactivity and expire after 12 hours; accounts lock after five failed sign-ins; a system-use notice is shown at sign-in; users can see and end their active sessions.
- **Privileged actions** (downloading installers, inviting users, changing MFA, platform administration) require a fresh MFA assertion, and Direwolf staff access to customer data is logged with the reason.
- **Passwords.** Onboarding is by invitation only, with no shared or temporary passwords. Passwords must be at least 12 characters and are screened against a breached-password list.
- **Operations.** Infrastructure is defined as code, reviewed in pull requests and deployed by digest; images and dependencies are scanned in CI.

This Website, by contrast, is a static site that holds no customer data and sets no non-essential cookies (today, no cookies at all); it is served over HTTPS and has a minimal attack surface.

No system is perfectly secure. If we confirm a security incident affecting your organization's data on Direwolf-controlled systems, we will notify your organization's administrators without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of confirming it, and we will notify affected individuals and regulators ourselves where the law requires us to (including under Colorado's breach notification law, C.R.S. 6-1-716).

### 11.2 Colorado residents

The Colorado Privacy Act (C.R.S. 6-1-1301 and following) gives Colorado residents acting in an individual or household context the right to confirm whether a controller is processing their personal data and to access it; to correct inaccuracies; to delete personal data; to obtain a portable copy; and to opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects. Direwolf does not engage in targeted advertising, sale or such profiling.

Direwolf may not currently meet the thresholds that make the Colorado Privacy Act apply to it, and the Act does not cover individuals acting in a commercial or employment context, which describes most people who use our Platform. We honor the rights above for Colorado residents regardless. To exercise them, contact us as described in 11.1. We will respond within 45 days, and may extend once by a further 45 days when reasonably necessary, telling you why. If we decline a request, we will tell you the reason, and you may appeal by replying to our decision with "Appeal" in the subject line; we will respond to the appeal within 45 days and, if we deny it, we will tell you how to contact the Colorado Attorney General. We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

### 11.3 California residents

**California Online Privacy Protection Act disclosures.** The categories of personal information we collect are listed in section 3, and the categories of third parties with whom it may be shared are in section 6. You may review and request changes to your personal information as described in 11.1. We notify you of material changes to this policy as described in section 14. Section 10 describes how we respond to Do Not Track signals and whether third parties collect information about your online activities over time and across sites (they do not). The effective date of this policy is shown at the top of the page.

**California Consumer Privacy Act.** Direwolf does not currently meet the revenue or data-volume thresholds that define a "business" under the CCPA. If that changes, we will add the notices the CCPA requires. In the meantime, California residents may make access, correction, deletion and portability requests as described in 11.1, and we do not sell or share personal information as the CCPA defines those terms.

### 11.4 Other states

Residents of other states with comprehensive privacy laws may exercise the rights in 11.1 in the same way. Most of these laws, like Colorado's, exclude individuals acting in a commercial or employment context; we honor the requests regardless.

When you use the Platform as a member of a customer organization, your organization is the controller of the personal information processed in its account, and Direwolf is its processor (or service provider). If you want to exercise rights over data your organization controls, please contact your organization's administrator first; we will support them in responding, and if you contact us directly we will refer your request to them unless the law requires us to act ourselves.

The data that sensors collect from your organization's devices is collected under your organization's authority and its own policies about monitoring. Your organization is responsible for telling its people about that monitoring in the way its policies and applicable law require.
